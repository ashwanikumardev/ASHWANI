Indian States and Their Unique Geographical Features

📅 September 26, 2026✍️ Insight Hub⏱ 11 min read

With 28 States and 8 Union Territories stretching across 3.28 million square kilometers, India presents one of the most astonishing geographic tapestries on the planet. Virtually every major landform found on Earth is represented within its boundaries.

From the world's highest salt desert in Gujarat to the living root bridges of Meghalaya's rainforests, let us embark on a regional tour highlighting the singular geographical marvels of Indian states.

Regional Geographical Highlights

Region Key States Distinctive Geographical Formations
Northern Himalayas Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal, Uttarakhand High-altitude cold desert (Ladakh), U-shaped glacial valleys, Karewa soil formations (Kashmir saffron)

1. Western and Arid Landscapes: Rajasthan & Gujarat

Rajasthan: Dominated by the ancient Aravalli Range—one of the oldest fold mountain systems in the world, heavily eroded into residual relict hills. West of the Aravallis lies the Thar Desert (Great Indian Desert), characterized by shifting sand dunes (*dhrians* and *barchans*), salty playa lakes (Sambhar Lake), and hyper-arid microclimates.

Gujarat: Boasts India's longest state coastline (1,600 km). Its most astonishing landscape is the Great Rann of Kutch—a seasonal salt marsh that transforms into an endless, blinding sheet of white salt crystals during the dry winter, and submerged wetlands during monsoons.

2. The Karst & Rainforest Wonders of Meghalaya and the Northeast

Meghalaya ("Abode of Clouds"): A plateau separated from the Chota Nagpur plateau by the Malda Gap. Composed of the Garo, Khasi, and Jaintia hills, it contains world-famous limestone karst caves (such as Krem Liat Prah) and the indigenous Jingkieng Jri—living root bridges trained by the Khasi people from the aerial roots of Ficus elastica trees across roaring jungle streams.

Assam: Defined by the mighty Brahmaputra river valley, home to the UNESCO World Heritage site Kaziranga National Park situated in the floodplains, and Majuli, the largest inhabited freshwater river island on Earth.

3. The Marine, Lagoon & Backwater Ecosystems of Kerala and Odisha

Kerala: Hugged between the towering Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea, Kerala features an intricate 900-kilometer network of brackish lagoons and lakes known locally as Kayals (such as Vembanad Lake). These interconnected waterways create a fertile coastal biome, including the Kuttanad region—one of the few places in the world where farming is practiced up to 3 meters below sea level.

Odisha: Home to Chilika Lake, Asia's largest brackish water lagoon, separated from the Bay of Bengal by a 60-kilometer-long sandspit. Chilika serves as a crucial wintering ground for over a million migratory birds and a haven for the endangered Irrawaddy dolphin.

4. The Deccan Volcanic Formations: Maharashtra & Madhya Pradesh

The Deccan Traps of Maharashtra were forged 66 million years ago by catastrophic volcanic fissure eruptions (the Reunion hotspot), creating step-like basaltic terraces and nutrient-dense black cotton soil (Regur). In Madhya Pradesh, the state forms the physical heart of India, where the Vindhya and Satpura ranges frame the rift valley of the sacred Narmada river.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Indian state has the longest coastline?+
Gujarat possesses the longest coastline of any Indian state, measuring approximately 1,600 kilometers with major natural harbors along the Gulf of Kutch and Gulf of Khambhat.
What are Karewas in Jammu and Kashmir?+
Karewas are lacustrine (lake) deposits of clay, silt, and sand found in the Kashmir Valley. These elevated flat terraces are globally renowned for cultivating premium quality Kashmiri saffron (Zafran) and almonds.
Where is the highest peak in South India located?+
Anamudi (2,695 meters), located in the Idukki district of Kerala within the Anaimalai Hills, is the highest mountain peak in South India and the Western Ghats.

Conclusion

The geographic versatility of Indian states underpins the country's rich agricultural output, climatic diversity, and cultural identities. Each landscape tells a billion-year-old story of geological upheaval, river deposition, and natural wonder.
